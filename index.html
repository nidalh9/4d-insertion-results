About the camera moves

  • The mark beside each clip is a top view drawn to scale: the square is the object, the dot is where the camera starts, the thick arc is how far it swings around the object, and a chevron means it also rises.
  • The camera always looks at the object. Each move is a new render of the same placed object; nothing is fitted again for it.
  • The size of each move is the largest that stayed inside the reconstructed scene on our checks: scene coverage per frame, the object kept in frame, and a by-eye look at the frames for floaters, holes and smears.
  • In most clips, scene splats closer to the camera than a fixed fraction of its distance to the object (0.2–0.6, set per clip) are not drawn.
  • Each object is reconstructed from one generated video, so a wide move also shows sides that video never saw, where the shape is weakest.

The captured scenes

  • Garden, Room, Stump and Counter: the Mip-NeRF 360 dataset; Room, Stump and Counter as the 3D Gaussian Splatting authors’ released models.
  • Playroom: the Deep Blending dataset, from the same release.
  • Coffee Martini and Cook Spinach: the Neural 3D Video dataset (CC BY-NC 4.0), as reconstructed by Mango-GS.
  • Juggle and Basketball: the CMU Panoptic Studio dataset, as reconstructed by Dynamic 3D Gaussians.
  • Reef: the SeaThru-NeRF Panama scene, as reconstructed in NerfBaselines.
